# Pilot Outreach Materials — Review Draft

**Status:** Content approved by Z on August 22, 2026 as offline pilot-support material. Not yet published, sent to an organization, or treated as a signed agreement or legal document.

**Working offer approved for this draft:** a no-cost, supported pilot with no preset end date for a known-membership organization of roughly 20–200 people, beginning with one to three meaningful but correctable decisions. Support is concentrated during setup and early use, then becomes lighter as the organization becomes self-sufficient. Continued use is welcome when the platform works for the organization.

## 1. Discovery-call guide

### Purpose

The first call is not a full product demonstration or a sales interrogation. Its purpose is to learn how the organization actually makes decisions, identify one safe and meaningful pilot use case, and determine whether there is a committed steward.

Recommended length: 30 minutes.

### Before the call

- Review the organization's public website and stated purpose.
- Record the contact's role and how they found Liquid Democracy.
- Have the demo organization open to one proposal that clearly shows direct voting, topic delegation, and overriding a delegate.
- Do not ask for a membership list, private dispute details, or confidential ballot information.

### Opening — 3 minutes

Suggested framing:

> Thanks for taking the time. I built Liquid Democracy because most organizations face the same tension: members want a meaningful voice, but very few people can follow every issue. The platform lets someone vote directly when they want to and delegate different topics to different people they trust. This conversation is mostly for me to understand how your group works and whether there is a useful, low-risk decision we could try together.

Confirm:

- Who is on the call and what role do they play?
- What made them curious enough to respond?

### Current process — 8 minutes

Ask:

1. What kinds of decisions does the organization make repeatedly?
2. Who is formally allowed to participate?
3. How are decisions made now: meetings, email, surveys, board votes, informal consensus, or something else?
4. What works well about the current process?
5. Where does it break down: turnout, meeting attendance, information overload, trust, speed, record keeping, or legitimacy?
6. Are results advisory, internally binding, or governed by bylaws/law?
7. Are there privacy, anonymity, accessibility, language, or technology-access requirements?

### Candidate pilot — 7 minutes

Ask:

1. What real decision in the next one to three months could be meaningful without being legally irreversible?
2. Could the group run a rehearsal decision first?
3. Approximately how many people would be invited?
4. Is membership known and can people receive individual email invitations?
5. Who would write proposals, answer member questions, and coordinate onboarding?
6. Would leadership be willing to tell members explicitly that this is a pilot and how the result will be used?
7. What result would make the pilot worthwhile even if the organization ultimately chose not to continue?

### Focused demonstration — 7 minutes

Show only this narrative:

1. A proposal has a topic and an appropriate voting method.
2. A member may vote directly.
3. The same member may delegate that topic to someone trusted.
4. They can override the delegate on one proposal or revoke the delegation entirely.
5. Public delegates can explain their positions; private delegation remains consent-gated.
6. Organization stewards configure membership, visibility, timing, and proposal rules.

Avoid touring every feature. Use later questions to decide whether identity verification, sub-organizations, role matrices, elections, stable-result rules, or advanced voting methods deserve a separate demonstration.

### Expectations and next step — 5 minutes

Explain:

- The service is pilot-stage and has no uptime SLA or election certification.
- It is not a conventional secret-ballot system; the operator can access the database, while ordinary member/org-admin visibility is restricted.
- The strongest pilot begins with a rehearsal and one to three real but correctable decisions, at a pace that fits the organization.
- Support includes setup, member material, and check-ins around the first decisions, then tapers as the organization becomes self-sufficient.
- The pilot is free, has no preset end date, and may continue if it works well. Any future pricing would require advance discussion and express agreement.
- No organization name, logo, quote, or result is used publicly without separate permission.

Then choose one outcome:

- **Strong fit:** schedule a setup/design call and send the Pilot Participation Understanding.
- **Possible fit:** request one missing decision or include an additional stakeholder.
- **Not now:** explain the boundary honestly and invite them to follow future progress.

## 2. Qualification checklist

### Strong-fit signals

- Approximately 20–200 known members.
- One clearly accountable steward and preferably a backup.
- At least one recurring decision type.
- A decision that matters but can be corrected or rerun.
- Leadership is willing to communicate the pilot honestly.
- Members can receive email invitations and use a modern web browser.
- The organization can run a rehearsal before the first real decision.
- Interest in participation quality and learning, not merely a new polling tool.
- The organization has time for a rehearsal and the first few decisions without a rigid or emergency deadline.

### Yellow flags requiring design discussion

- Fewer than 20 or more than 200 initial participants.
- Highly polarized internal conflict.
- Members expect the operator, org administrators, and infrastructure providers to be technically unable to associate accounts with ballots.
- Significant accessibility, language, low-connectivity, or device-access needs not yet tested with the cohort.
- Unclear authority to invite members or treat the result as meaningful.
- No backup steward.
- A hard deadline with no room for rehearsal or correction.
- Required identity verification involving minors or a jurisdiction-specific eligibility rule.
- Need for a complete automated data export or deletion guarantee before that process exists.

### Red flags for the initial cohort

- Governmental election or public-office vote.
- Legally mandated secret ballot.
- Contract ratification or other legally irreversible vote.
- Contentious officer election where a technical or procedural dispute would be high harm.
- Employment, housing, disciplinary, medical, or similarly sensitive individual decisions.
- Emergency or safety-critical decisions.
- The contact lacks organizational authority and cannot bring in someone who has it.
- The organization wants covert surveillance, forced disclosure, duplicate accounts, or manipulation of member voting power.
- No person will take responsibility for setup, communication, and support.

### Suggested scorecard

Score each 0–2:

| Dimension | 0 | 1 | 2 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Steward commitment | None | Interested but unclear | Named steward + backup |
| Member fit | Unknown/inappropriate | Edge of range or mixed access | Known 20–200 cohort |
| Decision fit | High-stakes/irreversible | Candidate needs reshaping | Meaningful and correctable |
| Timing | Immediate/rigid | Possible with adjustment | Rehearsal + paced early decisions |
| Transparency | Won't disclose pilot | Partial | Clear member communication |
| Learning value | Wants only a poll | One uncertain hypothesis | Clear governance question |

Interpretation:

- 10–12: strong fit.
- 7–9: follow-up/design needed.
- 0–6 or any red flag: do not accept as an initial pilot.

The score supports judgment; it does not replace it.

## 3. Two-minute pilot-video narration draft

This narration is intentionally short. Adjust to the final recorded screens rather than speaking faster.

### 0:00–0:15 — The problem

> Most organizations want members to have a real voice. But most members cannot attend every meeting, read every document, and become an expert on every issue. Traditional representation solves that by asking you to choose one person for everything. Liquid democracy gives you a more flexible choice.

### 0:15–0:35 — Direct voice

> On every proposal, you can vote directly. The organization chooses the timing, visibility, and voting method, and members can see the proposal, discussion, and result in one place.

### 0:35–0:58 — Delegate by topic

> When you do not want to follow an issue yourself, you can delegate that topic to someone you trust. That might be one person for budgets, another for operations, and someone else for community policy.

### 0:58–1:15 — Never surrender the vote

> Delegation never takes away your own voice. You can override your delegate on one proposal, change delegates, or revoke the delegation entirely whenever you want.

### 1:15–1:35 — Match the method to the decision

> Organizations can use simple yes-or-no votes, approval voting, ranked choice, multi-winner elections, or budget allocation. Public delegates can explain their positions and build an accountable record, while private delegation remains consent-gated.

### 1:35–1:48 — Organizational control

> Stewards control membership, topics, proposal rules, privacy settings, and whether identity verification is needed. We help the first pilot organizations choose a simple starting configuration rather than asking them to learn every setting at once.

### 1:48–2:00 — Invitation

> We are recruiting a small number of organizations for supported pilots. If your group has a real but correctable decision to make and wants to explore a more flexible form of participation, visit liquiddemocracy.us/pilot.

## 4. One-page member quick-start draft

### Welcome to your organization's Liquid Democracy pilot

Your organization is trying a different way to make decisions. You can participate directly when you want to and delegate by topic when you would rather trust someone else's judgment.

#### Your first five steps

1. Accept your individual invitation and verify your email address.
2. Confirm your display name and notification preferences.
3. Review the organization's topics and active proposals.
4. Vote directly on the rehearsal proposal.
5. If you want, choose a delegate for one topic and see how overriding or revoking that delegation works.

#### Three things to remember

- **You always keep your own vote.** A direct vote overrides a delegation for that proposal.
- **Delegations are topic-specific and revocable.** You can change them whenever you want.
- **Privacy has limits.** Ordinary members cannot see your vote by default, but this is not a conventional secret-ballot system. Public-delegate and approved-follower choices can make relevant activity visible, and platform operators can access the underlying systems.

#### During the pilot

- Ask your organization's steward how a result will be used.
- Report confusing instructions, accessibility problems, or unexpected behavior.
- Do not put confidential personal information into proposals, comments, or messages unless your organization has instructed you to and you are authorized to do so.
- Keep your password private and contact the support/steward address if you suspect account misuse.

The initial pilot does not include a complete automated export, a reusable portability package, or a supported self-hosting migration package. Those may be considered as future features if requested, but this memo does not promise them. Do not promise a deletion result beyond what the current Privacy Policy and available tools support.

## 6. Pilot check-in and continuation questions

Ask the steward:

1. How much work did setup and administration require?
2. Where did members need help?
3. Did invitations and email verification create drop-off?
4. Did members understand direct voting versus delegation?
5. Did members trust the privacy explanation?
6. Which voting methods were easy or confusing?
7. Did delegation change who participated or whose expertise mattered?
8. Were the results seen as legitimate and useful?
9. What support intervention mattered most?
10. Did the organization voluntarily run or schedule a second real decision?
11. Would it continue using the platform? Why or why not?
12. What would have to change before recommending it to another organization?

Measure without recording ballot contents or private proposal/member text:

- invitations sent, accepted, and verified;
- eligible members and participants;
- first direct vote and first delegation completion;
- number of support interventions;
- steward time estimate;
- rehearsal and real decisions completed;
- whether a second decision was voluntarily initiated; and
- continuation/referral intent.
